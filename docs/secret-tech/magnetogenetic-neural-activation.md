Magnetogenetics relies on the fusion of magnetic nanoparticles with mechanosensitive or temperature-sensitive ion channels on specific neural populations. The primary empirical pathways include:
- **TRPV4-Ferritin Fusion (Magneto2.0):** A synthetic gene construct that fuses the iron-storage protein ferritin to the transient receptor potential vanilloid 4 (TRPV4) channel. When exposed to an external magnetic field, the ferritin core experiences a magnetic torque, physically pulling open the TRPV4 channel and inducing calcium influx ($Ca^{2+}$) and action potential firing.
- **Superparamagnetic Iron Oxide Nanoparticles (SPIONs):** Nanoparticles (e.g., $Fe_3O_4$) functionalized with antibodies targeting specific membrane receptors. Under a high-frequency alternating magnetic field (AMF), SPIONs generate localized hysteretic heating, activating temperature-sensitive channels like TRPV1 without heating the surrounding brain tissue.

```
[External Magnetic Field] ---> [Ferritin Core Torque] ---> [TRPV4 Channel Opens] ---> [Ca2+ Influx] ---> [Action Potential]
```

AI-guided electromagnetic arrays focus magnetic gradients with millimeter precision through the human skull, targeting deep brain structures such as the nucleus accumbens (for addiction) or the subgenual cingulate cortex (for depression) without affecting adjacent tissues.

$$\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} I_i \mathbf{K}_i(\mathbf{r})$$
