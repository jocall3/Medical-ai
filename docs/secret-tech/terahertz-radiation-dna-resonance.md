## 3. Empirical Scientific Foundations
Terahertz radiation (0.1–10 THz, corresponding to wavelengths of 30 $\mu$m to 3 mm) occupies the spectral region between microwaves and infrared light. The low-frequency collective vibrational modes of the DNA double helix—specifically the hydrogen bonds holding the base pairs together—fall precisely within the THz frequency range. The empirical biophysical mechanisms include:
- **Resonant Hydrogen Bond Dissociation:** Specific THz frequencies can couple with the longitudinal acoustic modes of DNA, inducing local denaturation (unzipping) of the double helix without causing double-strand breaks. This exposes specific gene loci for transcription:

$$\omega_{resonance} = \sqrt{\frac{k_{H-bond}}{m_{eff}}}$$

- **Epigenetic Remodeling:** THz radiation can modulate the activity of DNA methyltransferases (DNMTs) and histone acetyltransferases (HATs), inducing targeted demethylation of promoter regions and reactivating silenced longevity genes (e.g., SIRT1, Oct4).
- **Non-Thermal Conformational Changes:** By utilizing ultra-short, picosecond THz pulses, we can induce conformational changes in chromatin structure without generating thermal energy, avoiding cellular damage.
