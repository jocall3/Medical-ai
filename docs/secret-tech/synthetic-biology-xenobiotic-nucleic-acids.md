## 3. Empirical Scientific Foundations
Xenobiotic Nucleic Acids (XNAs) are synthetic analogues of DNA and RNA in which the natural ribose or deoxyribose sugar backbone is replaced with alternative sugar moieties, or where the canonical bases (A, T, C, G) are replaced with synthetic hydrophobic base pairs. The primary empirical XNA chemistries include:
- **HNA (Anhydrohexitol Nucleic Acid):** Features a 6-membered anhydrohexitol ring, exhibiting extraordinary thermal stability and complete resistance to natural nucleases.
- **LNA (Locked Nucleic Acid):** Contains a methylene bridge connecting the 2'-O and 4'-C atoms, locking the ribose ring in a 3'-endo conformation, maximizing hybridization affinity to target RNA.
- **TNA (Threose Nucleic Acid):** Utilizes a 4-carbon threose sugar backbone, capable of forming stable antiparallel double helices and undergoing directed evolution.

Because natural polymerases cannot replicate or transcribe XNAs, AI models are utilized to design **orthogonal polymerases**—engineered enzymes that can selectively transcribe DNA into XNA, and replicate XNA templates, without interacting with the host cell's natural genetic machinery.
