### 2.1. DNA Methylation of the *INSR* and *IRS1* Promoters
In healthy tissue, the insulin receptor gene (*INSR*) and insulin receptor substrate 1 (*IRS1*) are actively transcribed, allowing cells to express highly sensitive surface receptors that bind insulin and trigger the intracellular glucose uptake cascade. In T2DM patients, chronic hyperinsulinemia and elevated free fatty acids (FFAs) trigger the upregulation of DNA methyltransferases (**DNMT1, DNMT3a, and DNMT3b**). 

These enzymes catalyze the transfer of methyl groups to CpG islands within the promoter regions of *INSR* and *IRS1*. This hypermethylation recruits methyl-CpG-binding domain proteins (MBDs) and co-repressor complexes, physically blocking transcription factors (such as **Sp1** and **HMGA1**) from binding. Consequently, transcription of *INSR* and *IRS1* is severely downregulated, leading to a drastic reduction in functional insulin receptors on the cell membrane of skeletal muscle, adipose, and hepatic tissues.

### 2.2. Histone Deacetylation and Chromatin Condensation
Histone acetylation, regulated by the antagonistic actions of histone acetyltransferases (HATs) and histone deacetylases (HDACs), determines chromatin accessibility. Acetylation of lysine residues on histone tails (e.g., H3K9ac, H3K27ac) neutralizes their positive charge, weakening their interaction with negatively charged DNA and creating an open, transcriptionally active chromatin state (euchromatin).

In T2DM, **HDAC1, HDAC3, HDAC4, and HDAC5** are pathologically overexpressed in peripheral tissues. HDAC5, in particular, physically interacts with the transcription factor **myocyte enhancer factor 2 (MEF2)**. Under resting or metabolic-stress conditions, HDAC5 deacetylates histones surrounding the promoter of the **SLC2A4** gene (which encodes the **GLUT4** glucose transporter). This induces chromatin condensation (heterochromatin), preventing the transcriptional machinery from accessing the gene. Even if insulin binds to the few remaining functional receptors, the downstream signal cannot trigger GLUT4 expression or translocation, trapping glucose in the extracellular space.

### 2.3. Non-Coding RNA and Epitranscriptomic Dysregulation
Beyond DNA and histone modifications, the AI Hospital identifies post-transcriptional epigenetic silencing mediated by microRNAs (miRNAs) and long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs). Pathological upregulation of **miR-29**, **miR-135**, and **miR-146a** directly targets the 3' untranslated regions (UTRs) of *INSR* and *IRS1* mRNA, promoting their degradation before translation can occur. Concurrently, the downregulation of the lncRNA **MEG3** impairs the epigenetic preservation of pancreatic beta-cell identity, accelerating beta-cell exhaustion and systemic metabolic collapse.
