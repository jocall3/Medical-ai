### Hashimoto’s Thyroiditis (HT)
Hashimoto’s thyroiditis is a T-cell-mediated autoimmune disease characterized by diffuse lymphocytic infiltration of the thyroid parenchyma, leading to the progressive destruction of thyrocytes and subsequent hypothyroidism.
*   **Immunological Drivers:** The disease is driven by a dominant Th1 and Th17 immune response. Autoreactive CD4+ T-helper cells recruit and activate CD8+ cytotoxic T-lymphocytes (CTLs) and B-cells.
*   **Molecular Targets:** The primary autoantigens are Thyroid Peroxidase (TPO)—an enzyme involved in thyroid hormone synthesis—and Thyroglobulin (Tg)—the precursor protein of thyroid hormones.
*   **Destructive Mechanisms:** Thyrocyte death occurs via perforin-granzyme-mediated apoptosis and the activation of death receptor pathways, specifically the Fas/FasL (CD95/CD95L) interaction. As thyrocytes undergo apoptosis, follicular structures are replaced by fibrous tissue and Hurthle cells (metaplastic thyrocytes rich in mitochondria), permanently destroying the gland's synthetic capacity.

### Graves’ Disease (GD)
Graves’ disease represents the opposite clinical spectrum of thyroid autoimmunity, characterized by hyperthyroidism due to the production of stimulating autoantibodies.
*   **Immunological Drivers:** GD is primarily a Th2-skewed autoimmune response that drives B-cell clonal expansion and the secretion of Thyroid-Stimulating Immunoglobulins (TSI).
*   **Molecular Targets:** The primary target is the Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone Receptor (TSHR) on the basolateral membrane of thyrocytes.
*   **Pathogenic Mechanisms:** TSI acts as an agonist, binding to the TSHR and mimicking the action of pituitary TSH. This induces chronic activation of the Gsα-adenylyl cyclase-cyclic AMP (cAMP) pathway, leading to unrestricted thyroid hormone synthesis, follicular hypertrophy, and diffuse goiter. Additionally, orbital fibroblasts expressing TSHR and IGF-1R are targeted by these antibodies, leading to the localized inflammation, glycosaminoglycan accumulation, and extraocular muscle swelling characteristic of Graves' ophthalmopathy.

### The Critical Role of CD4+CD25+FOXP3+ Regulatory T-Cells (Tregs)
Regulatory T-cells (Tregs) are the primary mediators of peripheral immunological tolerance. Characterized by the expression of the lineage-defining transcription factor FOXP3, Tregs suppress autoreactive T-cells, inhibit pathogenic B-cell activation, and limit tissue-destructive inflammation.
*   **Quantitative and Qualitative Deficiencies:** In both HT and GD, patients exhibit a marked reduction in the absolute number of circulating and intra-thyroidal Tregs, alongside functional impairment of their suppressive capacity. 
*   **The Th17/Treg Imbalance:** The loss of Treg-mediated suppression allows for the unchecked expansion of inflammatory Th17 cells. This altered Th17/Treg ratio drives chronic, self-sustaining inflammatory loops within the thyroid microenvironment, preventing spontaneous resolution and cementing the autoimmune state.
