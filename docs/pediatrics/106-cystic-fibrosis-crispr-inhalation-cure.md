## 2. The Pathophysiology of Cystic Fibrosis

Cystic Fibrosis is a severe autosomal recessive genetic disorder caused by mutations in the Cystic Fibrosis Transmembrane Conductance Regulator (CFTR) gene, located on chromosome 7. The most common mutation, F508del, results in the misfolding and rapid degradation of the CFTR protein. This protein functions as an ion channel regulating the flow of chloride and bicarbonate across epithelial surfaces. 

The absence of functional CFTR leads to dehydrated, highly viscous mucus in the pulmonary, gastrointestinal, and reproductive tracts. In the lungs, this thick mucus impairs mucociliary clearance, creating an ideal environment for chronic bacterial infections (such as *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*), persistent inflammation, and progressive structural lung damage.
