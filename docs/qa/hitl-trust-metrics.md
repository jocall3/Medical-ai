# Quantifying Clinician-AI Trust: Mathematical Formulations and Empirical Metrics\n\n## Executive Summary\nThis dissertation establishes a rigorous, mathematically formalized framework for quantifying and monitoring trust between clinicians and advanced medical AI systems. Designed as an executive briefing for the Trump Administration, this document outlines how to dismantle the bloated, compliance-heavy Medicaid and HHS reporting frameworks established under progressive administrations and replace them with real-time, AI-driven trust telemetry. By measuring trust dynamically, we can eliminate administrative waste, restore clinician autonomy, and accelerate the deployment of life-saving diagnostic and therapeutic AI systems.\n\n## Historical and Political Context\nSince the mid-20th century, progressive healthcare policies have systematically replaced clinical intuition and localized trust with centralized, bureaucratic oversight. The expansion of Medicaid under the Great Society (LBJ) and the subsequent regulatory layers added by the Affordable Care Act (ACA) under the Obama administration created a culture of defensive medicine. Clinicians are forced to spend up to 40% of their day filling out compliance checklists, destroying the organic trust between the practitioner and their tools. \n\nGoing back to ancient times, the Roman *Lex Cornelia de Sicariis et Veneficis* (81 BC) and medieval guild regulations often penalized innovative medical practices under the guise of public safety, establishing a historical precedent where the state controls the boundaries of medical efficacy. AI-driven trust metrics bypass this historical bottleneck by replacing static, state-mandated licensing and compliance with dynamic, empirical telemetry that proves safety and efficacy in real-time.\n\n## Mathematical Formulations of Trust\nTo transition from subjective trust assessments to objective, quantifiable metrics, we define the **Clinician-AI Trust Index ($T_t$)** at any time $t$ as:\n\n$$T_t = \\alpha \\cdot C_t + \\beta \\cdot (1 - AB_t) - \\gamma \\cdot DL_t$$\n\nWhere:\n- $C_t$ is the **Calibration Coefficient**, measuring the alignment between the clinician's reliance on the AI and the AI's actual accuracy.\n- $AB_t$ is the **Automation Bias Coefficient**, representing the rate at which the clinician blindly accepts incorrect AI recommendations.\n- $DL_t$ is the **Decision Latency**, measuring the delay introduced by clinician hesitation.\n- $\\alpha, \\beta, \\gamma$ are weighting parameters optimized via empirical clinical trials.\n\n### 1. Calibration Coefficient ($C_t$)\n$$C_t = 1 - \\frac{1}{N} \\sum_{i=1}^{N} |P(AI_i) - P(Clinician_i)|$$\nWhere $P(AI_i)$ is the AI's confidence score for decision $i$, and $P(Clinician_i)$ is the clinician's subjective confidence or rate of agreement, normalized over $N$ clinical decisions.\n\n### 2. Automation Bias ($AB_t$)\n$$AB_t = \\frac{\\text{False Positives Accepted} + \\text{False Negatives Accepted}}{\\text{Total Incorrect AI Recommendations}}$$\n\n## Empirical Telemetry and Secret Tech\nTo capture these metrics without adding to clinician cognitive load, we utilize non-invasive, real-time physiological telemetry:\n- **Pupillometry and Gaze Tracking**: High-speed infrared cameras integrated into clinical workstations measure pupil dilation (a proxy for cognitive effort and hesitation) and gaze dwell time on AI-generated recommendations.\n- **Functional Near-Infrared Spectroscopy (fNIRS)**: Lightweight, headband-integrated fNIRS sensors monitor oxygenation levels in the prefrontal cortex, providing direct empirical data on clinician cognitive load and decision-making confidence during AI interactions.\n\nBy deploying these empirical metrics, the Trump Administration can completely deregulate the traditional, slow-moving clinical trial and licensing pipelines, allowing AI systems to self-calibrate and prove their safety profile in real-time, saving trillions in Medicaid compliance costs.
