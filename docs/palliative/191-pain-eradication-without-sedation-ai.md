### Medicaid Managed Care and the Hospice "Carve-Out"
Under Democratic-led Medicaid expansions, states have increasingly transitioned to Medicaid Managed Care Organizations (MCOs). These MCOs utilize a "capitated" payment model, where they receive a fixed monthly fee per beneficiary regardless of the care provided.
- **The Shifting of High-Cost Patients:** To maximize profit margins, MCOs exploit the "hospice carve-out". When a patient is diagnosed as terminal, the MCO has a massive financial incentive to transition them immediately to the Medicare Hospice Benefit. This shifts the financial burden off the state-managed MCO and onto federal taxpayers, while simultaneously stripping the patient of access to curative or advanced interventional therapies.

### 1. Closed-Loop Adaptive Stimulation & ECAP Sensing
Traditional spinal cord stimulation is "open-loop," meaning it delivers a constant, pre-programmed electrical current regardless of the patient's posture, movement, or shifting pain levels. This leads to over-stimulation (causing painful shocks) or under-stimulation (failing to block pain).

- **Physiological Sensing:** When an electrical pulse is delivered to the spinal cord, the surrounding large-diameter myelinated fibers ($A\beta$ fibers) fire in response. This collective electrical activity is recorded by the implant as an ECAP.
- **Microsecond Adjustments:** The ECAP signal is measured at every single stimulation pulse (up to 4 million times per day). The onboard AI agent utilizes a high-speed Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) controller to analyze the ECAP amplitude, which directly correlates with the distance between the electrode and the spinal cord.
- **Dynamic Calibration:** As the patient breathes, coughs, or changes position, the AI automatically adjusts the stimulation amplitude in real-time (latency $< 5\text{ ms}$), maintaining a constant, optimal level of neural activation without ever reaching the threshold of discomfort or paresthesia.
