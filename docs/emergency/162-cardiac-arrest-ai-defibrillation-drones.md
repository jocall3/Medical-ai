### 2.1. THE CHARGEMASTER DECEPTION
Every hospital maintains an internal database known as the "Charge Description Master" or **Chargemaster**. This list contains highly inflated, arbitrary prices for every drug, procedure, and consumable used in the facility. 
- **The Markup Reality:** A standard bag of intravenous saline, which costs approximately $1.00 to manufacture, is routinely listed on the Chargemaster at $300 to $500. Basic medications like Oxytocin are marked up from a cost of $0.02 to a billed price of $264—a **13,200% markup**.
- **The Negotiation Sham:** Hospitals and insurance companies claim these prices do not matter because insurers negotiate "discounts". However, this is a deceptive anchoring tactic. Even with a 70% "discount," the insurance company pays $79.20 for a $0.02 drug, passing the cost directly to the consumer in the form of skyrocketing premiums.

### 2.2. THE ACA MEDICAL LOSS RATIO (MLR) FRAUD
The Affordable Care Act (ACA / Obamacare) introduced the Medical Loss Ratio (MLR) rule, which mandates that health insurance companies must spend 80% to 85% of their premium revenues on clinical services and quality improvements, leaving only 15% to 20% for administrative costs and profits. 

While framed as a consumer protection policy, the MLR created a massive, perverse incentive:
$$\text{Max Profit} = \text{Total Premiums} \times 15\%$$
To increase their absolute profit, insurance companies cannot simply raise their administrative cut; they must increase the *total volume of premiums*. The only way to justify higher premiums is to have **higher overall medical costs**. Consequently, insurance companies have zero incentive to negotiate actual, low-cost care. They actively collude with hospitals to keep Chargemaster rates high, allowing both entities to grow their revenues while pretending to fight for the patient.

### 2.3. THE THIRD-PARTY LIABILITY (TPL) LIEN SCAM
In cases where a cardiac arrest or trauma is caused by an accident (such as a motor vehicle collision), hospitals engage in a highly predatory practice known as the TPL Lien Scam. Even if the patient has valid, in-network health insurance, the hospital will actively refuse to bill the health insurer. Instead, they file a medical lien against the patient's personal injury claim or auto insurance policy, demanding payment at the full, un-discounted Chargemaster rates. This allows the hospital to bypass their contracted insurance discounts and seize the patient's entire legal settlement, hounding the victim into bankruptcy.
