### 7.1 Kolmogorov-Smirnov Drift Detection for Clinical Models
In a system managing life, death, and cryogenic resurrection, the AI must never operate on stale or drifted data. To detect when real-world clinical data begins to diverge from the distributions validated during simulation, the pipeline integrates real-time Kolmogorov-Smirnov (K-S) drift detection.

The K-S test is a non-parametric statistical test that compares the cumulative empirical distribution function $F_n(x)$ of the real-world clinical data stream against the baseline distribution $F_0(x)$ validated during the *in silico* trial stage. The test statistic $D_n$ is defined as:

$$D_n = \sup_x \left| F_n(x) - F_0(x) \right|$$

The pipeline continuously monitors $D_n$ for critical clinical inputs (e.g., blood glucose levels, cardiac rhythms, genomic mutation rates). If the test statistic exceeds the critical value $D_{\alpha}$ at a significance level of $\alpha = 0.01$:

$$D_n > \sqrt{-\frac{1}{2} \ln\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right) \frac{n + m}{n \cdot m}}$$

where $n$ and $m$ are the sample sizes of the baseline and real-world datasets, the pipeline triggers an automated rollback of the clinical model to the last known stable state and alerts the clinical engineering team.
