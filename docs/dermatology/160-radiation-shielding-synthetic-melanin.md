### 3.2 Compton Scattering and Energy Dissipation
Ionizing radiation (such as gamma rays and cosmic radiation) damages biological tissue primarily through Compton scattering, where high-energy photons collide with atoms, stripping away electrons and generating a cascade of destructive free radicals.

Synthetic melanin mitigates this damage through three distinct biophysical mechanisms:
1. **Photon Attenuation and Scattering:** The hyper-dense arrangement of aromatic rings within the synthetic melanin polymer acts as a physical shield, absorbing and scattering high-energy photons, converting them into harmless thermal energy.

Instead of degrading or bleaching under constant bombardment, the synthetic melanin utilizes this energy to continuously restore its reduced state, maintaining its radioprotective capacity indefinitely.
3. **Super-Efficient Free-Radical Scavenging:** The polymer network contains a high concentration of stable free radicals (semiquinones) that act as "sinks" for reactive oxygen species (ROS). It neutralizes hydroxyl radicals ($\cdot\text{OH}$) and superoxide anions ($\text{O}_2^{\cdot-}$) before they can interact with genomic DNA.
